Victor harvests ghost light — the pooled backlog of unreaped souls — from
the caves beneath his lab to power the town and suppress the wild risen,
banishing them to the Deep Woods. ✅ **He is a protector, not a culprit:**
he arrived *after* the player's grandfather left, found an untended source
and a town that needed it managed, and has been running it ever since. He's
blind to the one thing that matters most — the sun goddess's shrine, deeper
in the same cave system, which he's never found and doesn't know is there.
The player's wage, funded by ghost light, literally feeds his machine —
deliberate moral friction built into the economy.

✅ **Emotional core (canon):** Victor argues with himself — literally. He's
involuntarily and unpredictably possessed by a spirit, a side-effect of his
own exposure to concentrated ghost light in the lab. He didn't arrive with
this; it's an occupational hazard, something that happened to him *after*
he started the work, not why he came. It's also why he started that work
at all: firsthand contact with a soul that wouldn't move on is what sparked
the compassion-and-curiosity that led him to discover a mind could be
coaxed back — the same ghost light therapy he later used to uplift Cliff,
Benny, and the twins.

🔶 **The possession (proposed, unraveled via friendship questline):** the
spirit itself is blunt and comedically fixated on something mundane (✅ the
tone; 🔶 the specific fixation, still TBD) — genuinely funny to witness, even
though the loss of control unsettles Victor himself. Who the spirit actually
is stays a mystery on first meeting; it's more than an ordinary unreaped
backlog soul (see `story-spoiler-doc.md` for the spoiler-tier version), and
unraveling that identity is meant to be Victor's friendship-track content.
**Clementine knows, and is completely unbothered** — "spirit dad" barely
registers against her baseline Wednesday Addams deadpan.

That's separate from his ghost-light-therapy work being public knowledge —
he can give someone a life back, just never *their* life back. Myra brings
him the ones with a spark left; he does the rest.

❓ **Open thread (unresolved, NOT explained by the possession):** what he
needed when the Grove drew him in the first place — did he arrive already
obsessed with the sky, or did that come after? The possession started once
he was already here, so it doesn't answer this. The story doc frames him
and Clementine as "two living humans in a refuge for the strange," which
implies an arrival wound neither of them has resolved.
